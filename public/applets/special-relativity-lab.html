Left panel: the naive picture, everything Lorentz‑contracted along x at one instant of lab time. Right panel: what a distant camera actually records, because light from the far side of the object left earlier than light from the near side. The recorded image is identical to the undeformed object rigidly rotated by arcsin β. A sphere therefore always photographs as a circle, never as an ellipse.

Both clocks are identical: one photon bouncing between two mirrors, one tick per round trip. Switch the viewing frame and the roles swap exactly. Neither clock is really slow; each frame measures the other as slow, because "how many ticks so far" depends on which events that frame calls simultaneous. The twin paradox resolution lives in tab 2 — only the traveller changes frames, and that is what breaks the symmetry.
