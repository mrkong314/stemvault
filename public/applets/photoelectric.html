What breaks the wave theory. Classical physics says a brighter light delivers more energy, so electrons should always escape eventually — just sooner when the lamp is brighter. Drag intensity to maximum below the threshold. Nothing happens, at any brightness, for any waiting time. Then nudge frequency past f₀ and emission starts instantly, even at intensity 1.
Reading the graphs. The left plot's slope is Planck's constant — the same 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s for every metal. Changing the cathode slides the line sideways, never tilts it. Its x-intercept is f₀; its y-intercept is −φ. The right plot is a straight line through the origin: intensity sets how many electrons, never how fast.
